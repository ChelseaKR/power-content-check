# Internationalization

i18n status: N/A

Reason: this is an operator-facing command line tool with no rendered surface
and no end-user audience. Its input is a Power Content Label, a document
California regulation prescribes in English, and its output is a finding that
quotes English regulatory text back to the reader. Translating a finding while
leaving the quoted requirement in English would make the output harder to
verify against the source, not easier, and verifying a finding against its
source is the whole point of the tool.

There are no user-facing strings in the product sense: every string the tool
prints is either a citation, a quotation from a cited document, or a statement
about a specific English phrase that was or was not found in the label.

## What would change this

A Spanish-language Power Content Label. If the Energy Commission prescribes one,
the matching terms in `checks.py` become locale-dependent and this declaration
should be revisited along with them. Until then, adding a locale layer would
mean translating the operator's copy of an English legal requirement, which
adds a place for the requirement to drift from its source.

## What is already locale-safe

Text normalisation in `normalize.py` runs Unicode NFKC and folds the
typographic quote, dash and space variants that PDF extraction introduces, so
matching does not depend on which tool produced the document.
